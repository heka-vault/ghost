# Wake pipeline review: reSpeaker XVF3800 array, 2026-10-21

**Host:** i5-11600K, no GPU, PipeWire 1.6.2. **Mics:** the Logitech C615 webcam (what `ghost.mic` had been built on) and a Seeed reSpeaker XVF3800 4-mic array on its 2-channel 16 kHz USB firmware. Channel 0 (FL) is the array's processed beam (AEC, beamforming, noise suppression, AGC); channel 1 (FR) is its ASR beam or a mic's AEC residual, with fixed gain and no noise suppression.

**Path under test:** today's passive wake (ADR 0010), replayed offline with the same parts: Pipecat's Silero VAD with Ghost's parameters, `SegmentedSTTService` buffering (1 s pre-roll, segment ends at VAD stop, 0.5 s of silence appended), Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3 int8, `match_wake`.

**Clips:** 200 Kokoro clips in 16 voices: 80 wake ("Hey Ghost.", "Hey Ghost, what needs my sign off?", "Hey Ghost, any new issues today?", "Okay Ghost, read me the latest bug.", "Ghost, are you there?") and 120 negatives. Half the negatives are confusables chosen to trip the matcher ("Hey guys, what's up?", "He goes to the gym", "Hey Gus", "Okay, coast is clear", "Hey, goat cheese or cheddar?"). The "room" runs played a 100-clip subset (40 wake, 60 negative) through the desk speakers while every mic recorded at once. False wakes per hour come from LibriSpeech test-clean: 6.1 h of read English, five sentences of which mention ghosts.

**Not tested:** the operator's own voice. Synthetic speech from the desk speakers stands in for a person; the absolute numbers depend on where the speakers and mics sit. The comparisons between mics and settings hold; the operator's takes are the real test.

## Findings

1. **Ghost was not using the array.** `ghost.mic` was still built on the webcam, and nothing read the array. From the desk speakers the webcam heard speech only 3 dB above its noise floor; the array's channel 0 heard it 23 dB above.
2. **The fuzzy matcher's default (0.8) causes most false wakes.** On clean audio it added no recall over exact matching (78/80 either way) and woke on 15 of 120 negatives. On read speech it woke 2.1 times an hour, on "he set off", "her guests", "he sat down", "his gust" and "harrying our coast". At 0.9 it woke on nothing in any run (0/h, 0/60, 0/120) and kept the same-sound spellings ("he ghost") that exact matching misses.
3. **The passive VAD cuts phrases at commas.** At `vad_stop_secs` 0.2, "Ghost, are you there?" reaches the matcher as "Ghost are you." and "There.", and a bare "Hey Ghost" in its own short segment comes out as "He goes" or nothing. A passive-only stop of 0.5 s caught 4 more of 40 on the array, at 0.35 s more before the chime.
4. **A live wake-setting change crashed ghostd.** The passive loop cancelled the pending wake task, then asked whether it was done (a cancelled task is) and read its result. Every change to the wake phrases or sensitivity from the desktop killed the daemon, and systemd restarted it.
5. **A hold timer can outlive its session.** It was cancelled only by `hang_up` and resuming. A session ended any other way (the desktop's End, `/sleep`) left it running, and 30 minutes later it ran that session's hang-up, which marks the daemon's current state "Hanging up" whatever is live then. It fired twice in today's journal, both times while Ghost was passive.

## Results

Wake clips heard, out of 40, and false wakes on the 60 room negatives. Room, quiet, speech at full level from the desk speakers:

| Mic | Stop | fuzzy 0.8 | fuzzy 0.9 | exact | false wakes 0.8 / 0.9 / exact | "Hey Ghost." to wake, p50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| webcam C615 | 0.2 s | 30 | 24 | 20 | 6 / 0 / 0 | 0.21 s |
| array ch0 (FL) | 0.2 s | 34 | 29 | 29 | 6 / 0 / 0 | 0.25 s |
| array ch1 (FR) | 0.2 s | 32 | 32 | 31 | 8 / 0 / 0 | 0.23 s |
| array ch0 (FL) | 0.5 s | 36 | 33 | 33 | 9 / 0 / 0 | 0.56 s |
| array ch1 (FR) | 0.5 s | 36 | 35 | 33 | 8 / 0 / 0 | 0.58 s |
| array ch0, second run | 0.5 s | 36 | 34 | 32 | 8 / 0 / 0 | 0.49 s |

Over the same 6.1 h of read speech: fuzzy 0.8 woke 13 times (2.1/h); 0.9 and exact woke 0 times.

Other conditions:

- **Clean digital (no room):** 78/80 at every threshold. False wakes 15/120 at 0.8 and 0 at 0.9 or exact. Parakeet v2 (English only) did worse than v3: 75/80, with "a ghost" and "it ghosts" misses.
- **Music** (Spotify playing at speech level from the same speakers, no echo cancellation): webcam 7, ch0 7, ch1 11–12 of 40 at 0.9. This is the case the echo canceller is for: in `ghost.mic` the webcam/array signal has the desk sink subtracted, and in these recordings `ghost.mic` cancelled the played clips completely (0/40). Music or a TV that isn't on the desk sink is not cancelled, and only a trained detector with noise in its training data handles it well.
- **Far** (speech 13 dB quieter): below the room's own noise floor on every mic (about 2 dB SNR), so the VAD never opened. It says nothing about distance, only that the playback level was too low.
- **sherpa-onnx keyword spotting** (gigaspeech 3.3M, no training, boost 2.0, threshold 0.2): 63–68/80 on clean digital with 2–9 false wakes on the confusables; in the room 27/40 on ch0 and 10/40 on the webcam; 0 false wakes in 6.1 h of read speech; it fires about 0.3 s after the keyword. It is precise but misses too much to replace Parakeet. As a second opinion it helps: "exact, or fuzzy 0.8 that the spotter also heard" scored 35/40 with 0 false wakes on ch0.

The VAD never opened on room noise alone on any mic (no segments outside the clips).

## Options for the wake path

1. **Keep transcript wake, tuned (done today).** Array, strict fuzzy, a longer passive stop. On this replay it hears about 85% at the desk with no false wakes. The misses that remain are Parakeet turning a bare "hey ghost" into "he goes", which text matching can't tell apart from someone saying "he goes". It costs continuous Parakeet on every utterance in the room, and it only wakes at the end of the whole utterance.
2. **A trained "hey ghost" detector, with Parakeet as the verifier (recommended next).** This is how Alexa and Siri are built: a small always-on acoustic model fires a low-threshold first stage, and a bigger model checks the audio around the hit. `livekit-wakeword` (Apache-2.0, openWakeWord-compatible ONNX on onnxruntime, 0.3.0) trains a phrase from Piper TTS voices, room impulse responses and noise, with named confusable negatives ("hey guys", "he goes", "hey Gus"). It reports 0.08 false accepts per hour at 86% recall on its own phrase. Training needs about 16 GB of negative features and hours of CPU. In return, wake fires at the end of "ghost" (the chime can play while the operator is still talking) and decides on acoustics rather than text. Parakeet then runs only on hits, and the audio after the wake word can go straight to the session.
3. **Open-vocabulary keyword spotting (sherpa-onnx).** No training, but measured above: too many misses as a first stage. It's a usable stopgap verifier.
4. **Porcupine.** Out: its AccessKey is validated online and there is no personal tier after June 2026.
5. **The phrase.** "Hey ghost" is six phonemes, and its neighbours ("he goes", "hey guys", "he set") are everyday speech, which is what fuzzy 0.8 kept matching. With strict matching it made no false wakes in 6 h, so there's no need to change it yet. If a trained detector's false-accept rate is poor, compare three-syllable candidates with the same harness before choosing.
6. **The array itself.** Its AEC only works on audio played through its own USB output. Playing Ghost (and music) through the array's 3.5 mm jack would let the chip cancel it before the beamformer, and the host canceller could go. Its LED ring (`LED_EFFECT`, `LED_COLOR` over USB vendor control) could light on wake for Alexa-style feedback, and its direction-of-arrival readout could tell the operator from the speakers. Each needs the `xvf_host` control tool and a udev rule.
7. **The operator's own samples.** 50–100 takes of "hey ghost" through the array, from the desk, across the room and with music, are the test set that matters, and the validation set for a trained model. They should be recorded deliberately with `scripts/record-clip.sh`, never taken from passive audio.

## Changed

- Live on the workstation: `ghost.mic` captures the array's channel 0 alone (`GHOST_MIC_CHANNEL=FL`, new in `infra/pipewire/install.sh`); the webcam config is kept as `~/.config/ghost/echo-cancel.conf.webcam-2026-10-21`. `GHOST_WAKE_FUZZY=0.90` in the operator's settings.
- In the repo, live after the next package: `GHOST_WAKE_VAD_STOP_SECS` (0.5) for the passive listener only; fuzzy default 0.9 in ghostd and the desktop; the re-arm crash fix (`Daemon._passive`); the hold timer stopped however a session ends (`GhostSession.after_run`).

Not measured: whether the host canceller's own noise suppression and AGC help or hurt on top of the array's. The test instance dropped 11% of its audio under load, so its numbers were discarded. Channel 1 against channel 0 through `ghost.mic` is also untested; on raw recordings ch1 transcribed slightly better and ch0 was 12 dB cleaner and louder.
